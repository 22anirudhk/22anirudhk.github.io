CS 180 Programming Project #1

Images of the Russian Empire: Colorizing the Prokudin-Gorskii photo collection

Anirudh Kotamraju


The goal of this project was to transform the Prokudin-Gorskii photo collection into color images of 20th century Russia. For each subject environment, Prokudin-Gorskii captured the scene with three filters (red, green, blue), each onto a separate glass plate. Through image manipulation techniques to align each triplet of glass plate images, this project hopes to reconstruct what Prokudin-Gorskii once saw.

Captions show Red and Blue pixel displacements [row, column] relative to the Green channel.

Section 1: Single-Scale Alignment Results

I split each glass plate into its three channels. I decided to align the Red and Blue channels to Green by exhaustively trying small integer shifts (within ±40 px). The motivation for choosing alignment to the Green channel is discussed further in Section 2. One initial problem I faced was that some images had artifacts on their borders. I decided to solve this with cropping to prevent the alignment algorithm from getting drawn away from properly aligning the core/central part of the image. For each candidate shift I score similarity on an interior crop (~7% reduction on each top/bottom/left/right margin), so borders don't dominate. I decided to use Euclidean Distance (sum of squared pixel differences; lower is better) as my metric to score various alignments. Images are converted to float before scoring.

Section 2: Multi-Scale Pyramid Alignment Results

To handle larger displacements efficiently (crucial for the .tif files), I build an image pyramid by downsampling about 2x per level. I find a rough shift at the coarsest level, upsample that estimate, and refine it with a small local search at the next finer level—repeating until full resolution. The same scoring choice of Euclidean distance and the same interior crop (~7% reduction on each margin) are used at every level.

Initially, I aligned the red and green channels to the blue channel for each image. This worked for most images, except for emir.tif. This was one of the big problems I faced. Despite tuning my parameters for displacement search repeatedly, emir continued to be misaligned. In other words, the displacements for the red plate especially were unreasonably large. Then, I decided to align the red and blue channels to the green channel. This helped tremendously and allowed all three channels of emir to align well. My hypothesis is that this worked since unlike many of the other images, emir's blue channel intensity wasn't as proportional to the green or red channels.
