Image Warping, Feature Matching, and Autostitching

Anirudh Kotamraju

Overview & Pipeline

Part A stitches panoramas from hand-picked correspondences using inverse warps, nearest-neighbor/bilinear sampling, and alpha feathering. Part B replaces the manual step with Harris corners, ANMS, 40×40 to 8×8 descriptors, Lowe-ratio matching, and 4-point RANSAC to autostitch mosaics.

A.1: Shoot and Digitize Pictures

Each dataset was captured handheld with the camera pivoting about a fixed center of projection. I kept successive views closely spaced to preserve strong feature overlap.

A.2: Recover Homographies

I click correspondences, store them in JSON, and flip the saved (col,row) order into (row,col) when they are read back. Each match treats image 1 as the reference, so I build two rows of the linear system using the image 2 coordinates on the left and the image 1 coordinates on the right: [im2x, im2y, 1, 0, 0, 0, -im2x * im1x, -im2y * im1x] and [0, 0, 0, im2x, im2y, 1, -im2x * im1y, -im2y * im1y]. Stacking all rows gives an overdetermined Ah = b system for the eight free entries of the homography. I solve it with np.linalg.lstsq, append 1 as the bottom-right entry, and reshape the vector into a 3x3 matrix.

\[ \underbrace{ \begin{bmatrix} \mathrm{im2x}_1 & \mathrm{im2y}_1 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & -\mathrm{im2x}_1 \,\mathrm{im1x}_1 & -\mathrm{im2y}_1 \,\mathrm{im1x}_1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \mathrm{im2x}_1 & \mathrm{im2y}_1 & 1 & -\mathrm{im2x}_1 \,\mathrm{im1y}_1 & -\mathrm{im2y}_1 \,\mathrm{im1y}_1 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\ \mathrm{im2x}_n & \mathrm{im2y}_n & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & -\mathrm{im2x}_n \,\mathrm{im1x}_n & -\mathrm{im2y}_n \,\mathrm{im1x}_n \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \mathrm{im2x}_n & \mathrm{im2y}_n & 1 & -\mathrm{im2x}_n \,\mathrm{im1y}_n & -\mathrm{im2y}_n \,\mathrm{im1y}_n \end{bmatrix} }_{A} \underbrace{ \begin{bmatrix} a \\ b \\ c \\ d \\ e \\ f \\ g \\ h \end{bmatrix} }_{\mathbf{h}} = \underbrace{ \begin{bmatrix} \mathrm{im1x}_1 \\ \mathrm{im1y}_1 \\ \vdots \\ \mathrm{im1x}_n \\ \mathrm{im1y}_n \end{bmatrix} }_{\mathbf{b}} \]

\(\mathrm{im2x}_i\) and \(\mathrm{im2y}_i\) are the column/row of correspondence \(i\) in the second image, while \(\mathrm{im1x}_i\) and \(\mathrm{im1y}_i\) are the matching coordinates in the reference image.

\[ H = \begin{bmatrix} a & b & c \\ d & e & f \\ g & h & 1 \end{bmatrix} \]

I set the bottom-right entry to \(1\) after solving so the recovered vector reshapes cleanly into a homography matrix.

A.3: Warp the Images

For any warp I either reuse the object’s reference quadrilateral or map all four image corners through H to size the output canvas. Each pixel in the destination grid is sent through H⁻¹, divided by the homogeneous scale, and checked against the source bounds. Nearest neighbor simply rounds those coordinates, while bilinear interpolation uses the area-based weights from bilinear_interp_helper to blend the four neighbors. Results are clamped to uint8 before saving.

A.4: Blend Images into a Mosaic

I load RGBA inputs when available, recover H, and warp the partner frame with the bilinear routine. The warped correspondences are compared with the reference points to estimate a global row/column shift so both images land on the same canvas. Feathering relies on the alpha masks: distance_transform_edt produces smooth weights, I normalize the two masks so they sum to one per pixel, and then blend the color channels. I also dump each recovered homography to disk for debugging.

B.1: Harris Corner Detection

I reuse the provided Harris detector with a small Gaussian blur and apply adaptive non-maximal suppression to keep the 300 most spatially separated responses using an c = 0.9 threshold. The heat map shows the raw corner strength before suppression, followed by the overlays with and without ANMS.

B.2: Feature Descriptor Extraction

Around every ANMS corner I crop a 40×40 RGB window, blur it with σ = 1, subsample to an 8×8 descriptor using a stride of five pixels, and normalize each descriptor to zero mean and unit standard deviation. Below are several window/descriptor pairs sampled from the Berkeley Way West sequence.

B.3: Feature Matching

I compare descriptors with Euclidean distance, store the nearest and second-nearest neighbors, and keep matches whose distance ratio is below 0.8 (Lowe-style threshold). The figure shows a few matched patches plus the stacked image visualization of all surviving matches.

B.4: RANSAC and Automatic Mosaics

Four-point RANSAC samples correspondences for 6k iterations (7k for the house scene) and accepts inliers whose reprojection error is under the dataset-specific pixel threshold noted below. I recompute homographies from all inliers, warp the partner frame, and blend it with the same feathering from Part A. Each row compares the manual mosaic from Part A with the fully automatic result.
